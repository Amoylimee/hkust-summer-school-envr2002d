The bridge

Games led me to code. Code led me to research.

Coding changed me from someone who played with systems into someone who could build and analyse them.

09
Over time, gaming extended into curiosity about computers and then into writing code. Coding was the bridge. The research loop

Research gave me the same satisfaction as a difficult game.

A hard problem. Repeated attempts. Debugging. And finally, a pattern that makes sense.

10
This is what excited me. Every day, I could sit at a computer, write code, build algorithms and attack difficult problems. The first attempt often fails. You debug, rethink and try again. The reward is not winning. It is understanding the system a little better. [Sources] - Personal narrative supplied by Jeremy. - Image: AI-generated illustrative pixel art, 2026-07-30.
The fit

Shipping gave me the subject. Coding gave me the tools.

Research brought the two together.

11
This is the simplest way I can summarise my route. Shipping gave me a subject I already cared about. Coding gave me tools I genuinely enjoyed using. Research brought the two together. [Sources] - Personal narrative supplied by Jeremy. - Image: AI-generated illustrative pixel art, 2026-07-30.
What I do at HKUST

I use data to understand how maritime systems work.

Large-scale shipping activity data reveals patterns that are difficult to see from the shore.

12
Today, I work with large-scale global shipping activity data. From the shore, you see individual ships. In the data, you can begin to see the system: how vessels move, where activity concentrates and where environmental impacts may occur. [Sources] - Research description from Jeremy's public personal website and earlier TA presentation. - Image: AI-generated illustrative pixel art based on a maritime-data concept image, 2026-07-30.
The work in three moves

My research moves from observation to useful evidence.

Observe real activity. Interpret it with code. Translate the result into something decision-makers can use.

13
The technical methods can become complicated, but the logic is simple. First, observe the real system through data. Second, interpret it with code and algorithms. Third, translate the result into evidence that someone outside the research team can use. [Sources] - Research description from Jeremy's public personal website and earlier TA presentation. - Image: AI-generated illustrative pixel art, 2026-07-30.
What makes it meaningful

A paper is not the final destination.

The work matters when evidence enters a real conversation and supports a practical next step.

14
I enjoy the code, but I do not want the work to stop at the code or even at the paper. It becomes more meaningful when rigorous evidence enters a serious conversation with ports, policymakers and industry, and helps create a practical next step. [Sources] - Personal research framing supplied by Jeremy and earlier TA presentation. - Image: AI-generated illustrative pixel art, 2026-07-30.
After the PhD

I want to stay close to shipping—and closer to implementation.

I want to keep connecting research, public policy and industry action in the transition to lower-carbon shipping.

15
Looking ahead, I want to keep working where research, policy and industry meet. Shipping shaped my family and my education. Research gave me a way to contribute back to that industry. After the break, I want to show what that contribution can look like when science meets maritime policy.
