# Slide structure

This document records the approved narrative and the currently implemented
slides. Do not change the storyline without Jeremy's approval.

## Session frame

- Course: ENVR2002D, Class 9.
- Audience: undergraduate students with mixed backgrounds.
- Part 1: approximately 30–40 minutes.
- Break: Part 2 begins around 11:00.
- Part 2: approximately 40 minutes.
- Participation: optional, low-risk prompts only.

## Communication job

By the end of the lecture, students should understand how Jeremy arrived in
shipping research and how scientific evidence can support maritime
decarbonisation without determining policy by itself.

## Current visual rule

- white 16:9 canvas;
- black Times-style serif text;
- restrained dark-navy rules and labels;
- cover, overview and section divider are text-only;
- content slides may use text on the left and an image on the right;
- leave a slide text-only when no relevant visual is needed.

Part 2 contains fourteen slides.

| Slide | Narrative job | Visible copy / content | Visual treatment |

| 13 | Ask whether shipping warrants policy attention | **Is shipping a significant source of global carbon emissions?** Present four possible views about comparative priority, trade dependence, fleet scale and evidence before regulation; invite students to say which they find convincing. | One discussion prompt followed by four statement bullets. |
| 14 | Introduce the simplest emissions calculation | **How can we estimate carbon emissions from shipping?** Fuel consumption × emission factor; one tonne of marine diesel produces about 3.2 tonnes of CO2. | Large equation, one numerical example and a fuel-specific footnote. |

## Optional interaction points

- Ask students whether shipping is a significant global emitter, using road
  transport, aviation, trade dependence and fleet size as optional hints.
- Ask how fuel consumption could be estimated across a fleet of more than
  100,000 vessels, then introduce the bottom-up method.
- Use one optional show-of-hands question when comparing pathways.
- Ask whether a Green Shipping Corridor is a route, technology or coordination
  mechanism, then explain that it is primarily a coordination mechanism
  focused on a route.
- Ask why 84 active initiatives had produced only four realisation-stage
  projects, then use the feasibility wall to introduce route selection.

The lecture must still work smoothly if no student responds.
