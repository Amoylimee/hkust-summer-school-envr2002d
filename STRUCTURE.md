# Slide structure

This document records the approved narrative and the currently implemented
slides. Do not change the storyline without Jeremy's approval.

## Session frame

- Course: ENVR2002D, Class 9.
- Audience: undergraduate students with mixed backgrounds.
- Part 1: approximately 30–40 minutes.
- Break: Part 2 begins at 11:00.
- Part 2: approximately 40 minutes.
- Participation: optional, low-risk prompts only.

## Communication job

By the end of the lecture, students should understand how Jeremy arrived in
shipping research and how scientific evidence can support maritime
decarbonisation without determining policy by itself.

## Current visual rule

- white 16:9 canvas;
- black Times-style serif text;
- restrained dark-navy rules and labels;
- cover, overview and section divider are text-only;
- content slides may use text on the left and an image on the right;
- keep an image placeholder until a relevant visual is approved.

# Planned continuation

The remaining slides have not yet been implemented in the active Beamer deck.
The following sequence preserves the approved narrative while allowing the
exact page count to be refined.

## Part 1 — Remaining story

1. Shipping gave Jeremy the subject; coding gave him the tools.
2. Maritime data helps reveal how the shipping system operates.
3. Research moves from observation to evidence that decision-makers can use.
4. A paper is useful when it enters a real policy or industry conversation.
5. Jeremy wants to stay close to shipping and closer to implementation.
6. Transition: after the break, show what this contribution looks like in
   maritime decarbonisation.

## Part 2 — How science supports maritime decarbonisation

1. What science can contribute to maritime policy.
2. Why ship emissions must be estimated rather than directly observed.
3. The intuitive components of an emissions estimate.
4. The scale of global shipping emissions.
5. Comparing efficiency, operations, alternative fuels and port
   infrastructure.
6. Why technically attractive options can fail in practice.
7. Green Shipping Corridors as route-focused coordination mechanisms.
8. The actors and conditions that must align.
9. How maritime data identifies credible routes and commercial relationships.
10. The Hong Kong–San Antonio / Cherry Express commercial story.
11. How the research entered a policy conversation.
12. Closing synthesis: science informs policy, while implementation also
    depends on institutions, economics, politics, values and coordination.

## Optional interaction points

- Ask what information is needed before estimating emissions from visible
  ships, then answer immediately.
- Use one optional show-of-hands question when comparing pathways.
- Ask whether a Green Shipping Corridor is a route, technology or coordination
  mechanism, then explain that it is primarily a coordination mechanism
  focused on a route.

The lecture must still work smoothly if no student responds.
